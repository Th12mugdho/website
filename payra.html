Case study · Final Year Design Project

Payra

An autonomous hexacopter that delivers food and medicine in Dhaka without landing. It lowers a self-stabilising delivery box instead of touching down in crowded streets.

Validated in Gazebo / PX4 SITL Hardware assembly in progress Five-member team · BRAC University
01

Problem

Last-mile food and medicine delivery in Dhaka is slowed by traffic congestion and limited road access in dense neighbourhoods. Drones can skip the traffic, but landing one in a crowded street is often unsafe or simply impossible.

Payra explores a non-landing delivery model: the drone holds position overhead and lowers the payload in a smart delivery box, so it never has to touch down.

02

System architecture

03

Key specs

Platform
Hexacopter (6 rotors)
Flight controller
Pixhawk running PX4
Delivery method
Non-landing: payload lowered in a smart box
Payload control
ESP32 + IMU, active fan stabilisation
Software
ROS2 pipeline
Simulation
Gazebo with PX4 SITL, URDF/xacro airframe model
Team
Five members (FYDP)
Status
Simulation-validated; hardware being assembled
04

How it was validated

Before building hardware, the team validated the full system in simulation: flight dynamics, delivery-box behaviour and mission logic. It ran in Gazebo with PX4 software-in-the-loop (SITL) over a ROS2 pipeline.

The airframe was modelled in URDF/xacro for simulation.

05

Design report

Alongside the build, the team wrote a formal FYDP design report covering:

  • System specifications
  • Requirements engineering
  • Design constraints
  • Trade-off tables for conflicting requirements
06

Status

Payra is validated in simulation. Physical assembly of the hexacopter frame and the ESP32 delivery box is underway.
